Lower-body force is thought to underpin punching, but its association with punch impact force (PIF) in professional kickboxers, and its relation to body size, is unclear. In this exploratory cross-sectional study, 14 Japanese professional male kickboxers (age $24 \pm 4$ years; body mass (BM) $69.1 \pm 4.4$ kg) were tested in the off-season on a force platform during the countermovement jump (CMJ), isometric mid-thigh pull (IMTP) and a standardized rear-hand cross, with PIF measured through a foam-and-glove interface. Gross CMJ peak force ($r = 0.71$, $p = 0.005$), gross IMTP peak force ($r = 0.55$, $p = 0.041$) and BM ($r = 0.67$, $p = 0.009$) were each positively associated with PIF (Holm-adjusted $p = 0.014$, $0.041$ and $0.017$). Together they explained 64.7% of PIF variance in-sample (adjusted $R^2 = 0.541$; leave-one-out cross-validated $R^2 = 0.427$), but no predictor was independently significant once BM entered the model (all $p \geq 0.100$). Scaling CMJ force to body size weakened its association with PIF (ratio: $r = 0.38$, 95% CI $[-0.19, 0.76]$, $p = 0.182$; allometric: $r = 0.52$, 95% CI $[-0.01, 0.82]$, $p = 0.057$), whereas net CMJ force remained associated with PIF ($r = 0.64$, 95% CI $[0.16, 0.87]$, $p = 0.014$). The CMJ correlation did not differ significantly from the IMTP ($p = 0.428$) or BM ($p = 0.847$) correlations. Confidence intervals are wide, so replication is needed.
