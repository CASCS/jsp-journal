Lower-body force is thought to underpin punching, but its association with punch impact force (PIF) in a
homogeneous group of professional kickboxers, and its relation to body size, is unclear. Fourteen Japanese
professional male kickboxers (age 24±4 years; body mass (BM) 69.1±4.4 kg) were tested on a force platform
during the countermovement jump (CMJ), isometric mid-thigh pull (IMTP) and a standardized rear-hand
cross. CMJ peak force (𝑟 = 0.79, 𝑝 = 0.001), IMTP peak force (𝑟 = 0.60, 𝑝 = 0.025) and BM (𝑟 = 0.63,
𝑝 = 0.015) were each positively associated with PIF, and together explained 78.1% of its variance (adjusted
𝑅2 = 0.715). Only CMJ peak force remained significant in the regression, and this changed when individual
athletes were excluded. CMJ peak force was therefore the most consistent correlate of rear-hand PIF in this
cohort, whereas the independent contributions of IMTP force and BM were unstable. Confidence intervals
are wide, so replication is needed.
