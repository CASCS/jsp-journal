* **Background.** Lower-body force production is considered central to punching, but its association with punch impact force in a homogeneous group of trained kickboxers has not been well quantified. 
* **Methods.** In a cross-sectional design, 14 Japanese professional male kickboxers from K-1 Japan weight categories (age 24 ± 4 years; body mass 70.7 ± 8.6 kg; ≥3 years of competitive experience) completed countermovement jump (CMJ) and isometric mid-thigh pull (IMTP) testing on a force platform and a standardized rear-hand punch (cross) impact protocol. 
* **Results.** CMJ peak force (r = 0.71, 95% CI \[0.28, 0.90], p = 0.005) and IMTP peak force (r = 0.57, 95% CI \[0.05, 0.84], p = 0.034) were positively associated with punch impact force. Together they accounted for 59.8% of the variance (adjusted R² = 0.52; F(2,11) = 8.17, p = 0.007); IMTP added no significant increment beyond CMJ (ΔR² = 0.10, p = 0.127). 
* **Conclusion.** Higher lower-body peak force was associated with greater rear-hand punch impact force in this cohort. Confidence intervals are wide and one athlete was influential, so the findings require replication.
