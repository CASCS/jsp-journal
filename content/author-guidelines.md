The journal, from the second half of 2023, publishes only short, single-claim research papers in which every statement is traceable to its data and methods; these guidelines say what to include so reviewers can verify your claim.

## Scope and article types

The Journal of Strength and Performance is an open access journal that provides a forum for experimental findings that usually remain hidden: the incremental steps that are so important to experimental research. These results often go unpublished because, in the traditional scholarly communication process, only a select group of experiments is chosen to make up the narrative of a single paper.

The journal is non-profit and diamond open access: reading and publishing are free. It accepts short papers that argue one principal claim with its supporting evidence. Articles for consideration include:

- **Positive results:** findings that support the hypothesis, reported in full.
- **Negative or null results:** findings that do not support the hypothesis or show no effect.
- **Exploratory studies:** preliminary or hypothesis-generating work, labeled as exploratory.
- **Reproducibility studies:** replications of published findings, whether or not they agree with the original.
- **Supplementary findings:** results that did not fit the narrative of a larger paper but are worth reporting on their own.

The journal does not consider papers that bundle several unrelated claims, narrative reviews without a new dataset, or work that cannot be described with full methods.

## Manuscript structure

Write the manuscript in English. Use the sections below in this order. Each section has one job: the reviewer should be able to follow your claim from the first sentence to the data behind it.

1. **Title.** State the population, the measures and the design. Do not use causal verbs ("drives", "determines") unless the design is experimental.
2. **Authors and affiliations.** Give names as given name(s) and family name(s) separately. Anonymize the manuscript file itself (see Submission).
3. **Principal Claim.** One standalone sentence, quantitative or mechanistic, with its scope inside the sentence: who, what was measured, what was found, and how large. Example form: "In 14 male amateur fighters, peak force in the isometric mid-thigh pull was associated with rear-hand punch impact force (r = 0.71, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.90)."
4. **Context.** The specific gap, in a few sentences, with the citations the gap depends on. Cite the finding you rely on, not just the paper. Mention competing or contradicting findings.
5. **Methods.** Enough detail for another laboratory to repeat the work (see Reporting standards).
6. **Results and Evidence.** Report every number the claim depends on: effect size, confidence interval and exact p-value. Figures show individual data points or variability, with units on both axes. Numbers in the text, tables and figures must agree.
7. **Interpretation.** State what the data show within the design. Correlational or cross-sectional data support association, not causation or training prescriptions. Name the most credible alternative explanation and say why it does or does not account for the result.
8. **Practical Significance.** Say who can use the result (coaches, clinicians, researchers) and how, in proportion to the evidence.
9. **Limitations.** List the limits specific to this study: sample, selection, design, measurement error, and what the data cannot answer.
10. **Declarations.** Ethics approval and consent, funding, conflicts of interest, author contributions, data and code availability.
11. **References.** Every reference cited in the text appears in the list and the reverse. Include a DOI or stable link where one exists.

Keep the paper short. A concise paper that supplies everything the claim needs is preferred over a long one.

## Conditions of submission

The Journal of Strength and Performance considers all manuscripts on these strict conditions:

- The manuscript is your own original work and does not duplicate any previously published work.
- All co-authors approve the submission and have contributed sufficiently to the research.
- The manuscript has been submitted only to the Journal of Strength and Performance and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere.
- Where applicable, the institution where the work was carried out has approved the research.

The publisher is not legally responsible for any claims for compensation concerning these requirements. By submitting a manuscript, you agree to any originality checks it may undergo during peer review and production. Any author who fails to adhere to these conditions is rejected.

## Ethics and research integrity

**Publication ethics.** The journal follows the principles of the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE), including its guidelines on the peer review process and its guidelines for reviewers.

**Ethical approval.** Every manuscript reporting research on human subjects must include a statement that the author's institutional review committee for human subjects approved the protocol. Papers that do not agree with the latest revision of the Declaration of Helsinki are not accepted. Participation must be voluntary and based on signed informed consent. Do not use any designation in tables, figures or photographs that identifies a participant or patient, unless you submit the person's written consent.

**Plagiarism.** The journal does not tolerate plagiarism of any kind: direct plagiarism, self-plagiarism, patch writing or accidental plagiarism. Submitted manuscripts are screened with Plagiarism Checker X for similarity with previously published material. Every suspected case is investigated thoroughly. For evident plagiarism, the work is rejected and the author's superiors are informed.

## Submission

Submit through the submission form on the journal website. The form has two parts:

1. **Author details.** Names (given and family names separately), affiliations, corresponding author, declarations. The editor sees these; reviewers do not.
2. **Anonymized manuscript.** Upload the manuscript as a file with no author names, affiliations, acknowledgments or funding details. Cite your own earlier work in the third person ("Smoak (2023) found…"), and remove identifying information from file properties and from repository links.

Acknowledgments, funding and the author list are added back after acceptance. Submit each manuscript once; do not submit work that is under review elsewhere.

## Peer review

The journal uses double-blind review: reviewers do not know the authors, and authors do not know the reviewers. Reviewers follow the claim through its support: the principal claim, its qualifiers, the data behind each part of it, the methods that produced the data, the references it relies on, and the challenges to it.

Reviewers assess five dimensions:

1. **Title and Principal Claim:** the title matches the scope, and the claim is a clear standalone sentence with its limits stated.
2. **Context and Rationale:** a specific gap, concise background, and citations that show what the manuscript says they show.
3. **Methods and Transparency:** complete sample, equipment and protocol detail, reported reliability, and statistics that fit the design.
4. **Evidence and Interpretation:** numbers agree across text, tables and figures; conclusions stay within the design.
5. **Practical Significance and Limitations:** applications proportionate to the evidence, specific limitations, and competing explanations addressed.

Reviewers also recompute the statistics your claim depends on from the numbers you report. Check your own arithmetic before submitting.

Each report ends with one decision. You will receive the reviews with the decision, and every critique is marked **Required** (it changes the decision) or **Suggested**.

### Process and timeline

1. **Editorial screening.** An editor checks that the topic fits the aims and scope of the journal and that the manuscript format guidelines are met. A manuscript outside the scope, or one that does not follow the format guidelines, is not accepted.
2. **Peer review.** Two reviewers each send a recommendation to the handling editor. If their opinions differ, the editor asks a third reviewer.
3. **Outcome.** The manuscript is rejected with a detailed letter, or accepted with minor or major revisions. For revisions you receive a report with the required changes and resubmit the manuscript. When the review requires no more amendments, the manuscript is accepted and sent to production.

Return a revised manuscript within 10 days at most; otherwise the process stops and the article may be rejected. An accepted article usually takes less than 45 days from submission to publication, depending on how quickly authors and reviewers respond. A manuscript that is not accepted is always returned with the comments of the editor and the reviewers.

## Publication, open access and identifiers

The journal does not charge article processing charges (APCs), submission charges or subscription fees. It is open access, and authors keep full ownership of the content they publish. Articles are published under the Creative Commons Attribution License (CC-BY), which allows anyone to reuse and redistribute them without restriction, as long as the original work is correctly cited.

Each accepted article is deposited in the journal's community on Zenodo and receives two persistent identifiers: a Zenodo DOI and an ARK (resolved through n2t.net). Both appear on the article page, and the article's short web address is based on the end of its Zenodo DOI. Cite articles with the author's family name and initials, then the year, for example "Smoak, Y. (2023)".

After acceptance you will receive a proof of the article page and PDF. Corrections after publication are made as a dated correction notice linked to the original article.

## Self-archiving and privacy

**Self-archiving.** You are permitted and encouraged to post your published article on your personal or institutional website. State clearly that the paper was published in the Journal of Strength and Performance, and link to the journal's site.

**Privacy.** The names and email addresses entered on the journal website are used only for the stated purposes of the journal and are not made available for any other purpose or to any other party.

## Pre-submission checklist

Tick each item before you submit.

- [ ] The Principal Claim is one standalone sentence, with population, measure and size of the effect in it.
- [ ] Title, abstract, claim and conclusion say the same thing at the same strength.
- [ ] Wording matches the design: association for correlational data, no prescriptions without intervention data.
- [ ] Sample details, equipment, protocol and reliability are all reported.
- [ ] Every statistic the claim depends on has an effect size, exact p-value and 95% confidence interval, and I recomputed it.
- [ ] Numbers agree across text, tables and figures; figures show variability and units.
- [ ] Each key citation shows what I say it shows; none rests only on a narrative review or on my own pilot work.
- [ ] I named the strongest alternative explanation and the main confounders.
- [ ] Limitations are specific to this study.
- [ ] Ethics approval, consent, funding, conflicts, contributions and data availability are declared.
- [ ] The manuscript file contains no names, affiliations or identifying file properties.
- [ ] All co-authors approved the submission and contributed sufficiently to the research.
- [ ] The manuscript is original, duplicates no published work, and is not under consideration elsewhere.
- [ ] The manuscript states institutional ethics approval and signed informed consent, and identifies no participant.
- [ ] I will return any requested revision within 10 days.
